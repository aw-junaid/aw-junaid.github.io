Every differentiation rule and common derivative you'll actually reach for – the limit definition, the core rules, and quick-reference tables for trig, inverse trig, exponential, logarithmic, and hyperbolic functions.

01.The Limit Definition

Every derivative rule below is just a shortcut for this one definition – the instantaneous rate of change of a function at a point.

equation — derivative_definition
f'(x) = limh→0   [ f(x + h) − f(x) ] / h

02.Basic Differentiation Rules 7 rules

Constant Rule
d/dx [c] = 0
The derivative of any constant is zero – a flat line has zero slope everywhere.
Power Rule
d/dx [xn] = n·xn−1
Bring the exponent down as a coefficient, then subtract one from the exponent.
Constant Multiple Rule
d/dx [c·f(x)] = c·f'(x)
Constants factor straight out of the derivative.
Sum / Difference Rule
d/dx [f(x) ± g(x)] = f'(x) ± g'(x)
Differentiate term by term.
Product Rule
d/dx [f(x)·g(x)] = f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)
"First times derivative of second, plus second times derivative of first."
Quotient Rule
d/dx [f(x)/g(x)] = [f'(x)g(x) − f(x)g'(x)] / [g(x)]2
"Low d-high minus high d-low, over the square of what's below."
Chain Rule
d/dx [f(g(x))] = f'(g(x))·g'(x)
Derivative of the outer function (leaving the inner alone), times the derivative of the inner function.

07.Tips & Common Mistakes

Implicit differentiation:when y is defined implicitly in terms of x, differentiate both sides with respect to x and treat dy/dx as an unknown to solve for – remember to multiply by dy/dx every time you differentiate a y term.
Higher-order derivatives:f''(x) is the derivative of f'(x), f'''(x) is the derivative of that, and so on – just apply the same rules again to the previous result.
Common mistake — product vs. chain:d/dx[sin(x)·cos(x)] needs the product rule; d/dx[sin(cos(x))] needs the chain rule. Mixing the two up is the single most common differentiation error.
Common mistake — the quotient rule order:the numerator is f'g − fg', not fg' − f'g. Getting the subtraction order backwards silently flips the sign of the whole answer.
