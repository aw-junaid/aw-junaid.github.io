01.Ohm's Law & Power

The relationship between voltage, current, and resistance – the single most-used formula in electronics.

equation — ohms_law
V = I × R
V = I × R
Voltage = current × resistance.
I = V / R
Current = voltage ÷ resistance.
R = V / I
Resistance = voltage ÷ current.
P = V × I
Power = voltage × current.
P = I² × R
Power in terms of current and resistance.
P = V² / R
Power in terms of voltage and resistance.

03.Series & Parallel Combinations

Resistors / Inductors in Series
Rtotal = R1 + R2 + ... + Rn
Same rule applies to inductors: Ltotal = L1 + L2 + ...
Resistors / Inductors in Parallel
1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ... + 1/Rn
For exactly two: Rtotal = (R1×R2) / (R1+R2). Same form applies to inductors.
Capacitors in Series
1/Ctotal = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ... + 1/Cn
Capacitors combine the opposite way to resistors – series capacitance is always less than the smallest individual capacitor.
Capacitors in Parallel
Ctotal = C1 + C2 + ... + Cn
Parallel capacitance simply adds, like resistors in series.

04.Voltage & Current Dividers

Voltage Divider
Vout = Vin × R2 / (R1 + R2)
Vout is measured across R2, the resistor closer to ground.
Current Divider (two branches)
I1 = Itotal × R2 / (R1 + R2)
Note the resistor in the numerator is the one NOT carrying the current you're solving for.

05.RC & RL Time Constants

How quickly a capacitor or inductor charges and discharges through a resistor.

τ = R × C
RC time constant, in seconds (R in ohms, C in farads).
τ = L / R
RL time constant, in seconds (L in henries, R in ohms).
Vc(t) = V(1 − e−t/τ)
Capacitor voltage while charging toward V.
Vc(t) = V × e−t/τ
Capacitor voltage while discharging from V.

After 1τ, a capacitor reaches about 63% of full charge (or decays to 37%). It's considered fully charged/discharged after roughly 5τ.

06.AC, Reactance & Resonance

XC = 1 / (2πfC)
Capacitive reactance – decreases as frequency increases.
XL = 2πfL
Inductive reactance – increases as frequency increases.
fr = 1 / (2π√(LC))
Resonant frequency of an LC circuit, where XL = XC.
VRMS = Vpeak / √2
RMS value of a sine wave – what a multimeter typically reports.

11.Tips & Safety Notes

Kirchhoff's Laws:KCL – the sum of currents into a node equals the sum flowing out. KVL – the sum of voltage drops around any closed loop equals zero. Almost every circuit analysis technique builds on these two.
Decoupling capacitors:place a small ceramic capacitor (commonly 0.1μF) close to each IC's power pin to filter noise – a common source of mysterious digital glitches when omitted.
Common mistake — LED without a resistor:LEDs have almost no internal resistance; connecting one directly across a supply above its forward voltage draws excessive current and destroys it almost instantly.
Safety — mains and high voltage:treat anything connected to mains AC as lethal until verified otherwise with a meter. Capacitors in power supplies can hold a dangerous charge even after the circuit is unplugged – discharge them safely before working on the board.
