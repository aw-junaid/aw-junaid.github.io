PDF to Word Converter

Most "PDF to Word" tools just dump every line as a separate paragraph and call it a day. This one actually rebuilds the document: it detects headings from font size, keeps bold/italic, turns bullet and numbered lines back into real Word lists, reconstructs tables from column alignment, pulls out embedded images, keeps hyperlinks clickable, recognizes repeating headers/footers and page numbers, and can OCR scanned pages that have no text layer at all.
The real-world problem this solves

PDFs don't store paragraphs, lists, or tables — they store positioned glyphs. Naive converters lose structure entirely: headings become body text, two-column layouts get scrambled, tables turn into tab-separated mush, and scanned contracts convert to a blank page because there's no text layer to extract. This tool reconstructs structure from glyph position/size heuristics, falls back to OCR when no text exists, and shows you exactly what it detected before you download.

✦ How This Tool Works

Why doesn't a PDF just "have" paragraphs already?

A PDF stores glyphs at exact x/y coordinates, not document structure — there's no native concept of a paragraph, heading, or list. This tool reconstructs that structure by clustering text into lines by vertical position, grouping lines into paragraphs by the gap between them, and inferring headings, lists, and tables from font size, spacing, and column alignment.

Reflow vs. Preserve Layout — which should I use?

Reflow rejoins wrapped lines into normal editable paragraphs and is right for single-column documents like reports and letters. Preserve Layout keeps each line separate, which holds up better on multi-column pages, forms, and resumes where Reflow would merge unrelated columns into nonsense sentences.

How does table detection actually work?

The engine looks for consecutive lines where text clusters into the same set of horizontal gap positions — a strong signal of column alignment. When that pattern holds across multiple rows it rebuilds a real Word table with matching cells rather than exporting the columns as tab-separated text.

What happens with a scanned PDF that has no text at all?

Pages under the character threshold are flagged as likely scanned. If OCR is enabled, that page is rendered to a canvas and run through Tesseract.js entirely in your browser to recognize the text, which is then inserted as normal editable paragraphs and noted in the conversion report.

Will bold, italic, and hyperlinks survive?

Bold and italic are inferred from the embedded font's name, which works well for most PDFs but isn't guaranteed for every producer — treat it as best-effort. Hyperlink annotations are matched to the text they overlap and exported as real clickable Word hyperlinks.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. Parsing, structure detection, OCR, and .docx generation all happen locally in your browser using pdf.js, Tesseract.js, and docx.js — your file is never sent anywhere, which matters most for exactly the sensitive documents this tool tends to be used on.
