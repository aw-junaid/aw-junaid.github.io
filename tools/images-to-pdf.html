✦ How This Tool Works

Why do my phone photos come out sideways in other converters?

Phones save the pixel data upright but store the rotation as EXIF metadata rather than rotating the pixels — tools that ignore that tag show the photo sideways. This converter reads the EXIF orientation tag and rotates the pixels before laying out the page, so portrait selfies and sideways landscape shots both come out the right way up.

My PDF is huge — how do I shrink it?

Two settings do the heavy lifting: "Max long edge" downsizes any photo bigger than your target resolution before it's embedded, and JPEG quality controls compression on top of that. A 12MP phone photo at quality 85 and a 2400px cap typically drops to a fraction of its original size with no visible difference on screen.

What's the difference between "Fit", "Fill", and "Actual size"?

Fit shows the whole image with no cropping, leaving background around it if the shape doesn't match the page. Fill crops the image so it covers the entire cell edge-to-edge. Actual size places the image at its real-world print size (based on the assumed source DPI) without scaling it up or down.

When would I use "Images per page" above 1?

A grid of 4, 6, or 9 is built for contact sheets, receipt bundles, or evidence photo sets where reviewers want to scan many images quickly rather than flip one page at a time. Combine it with captions so each thumbnail keeps its original filename.

Can I get a separate PDF for each image instead of one combined file?

Yes — switch Output File to "One PDF per image" and the tool packages every page as its own PDF inside a single .zip download, useful when each image needs to be filed or emailed separately.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. Every image is read, rotated, compressed, and assembled into a PDF locally in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere. Reload the page and everything is cleared, except your settings, which are remembered for next time.
