✦ How This Tool Works

How does a browser tool do a DNS lookup?

It uses DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) — a plain HTTPS request to a public resolver (Cloudflare or Google, your choice in settings) that returns the DNS answer as JSON. It's the same underlying data dig TXT example.com would return, just fetched over HTTPS instead of port 53 so it works from a browser.

Why does the SPF lookup count matter?

RFC 7208 caps SPF evaluation at 10 DNS-lookup mechanisms (include, a, mx, ptr, exists, redirect) — go over that limit and receiving mail servers are supposed to treat the whole record as a PermError, which can silently break delivery. Each extra mail vendor added to an SPF record (include:vendor.com) usually costs at least one lookup, so this is easy to exceed after a few integrations.

What does a DMARC policy of p=none actually do?

p=none tells receiving servers to take no enforcement action on mail that fails SPF/DKIM alignment — it only asks for reports (if rua= is set). It's the standard first step before moving to p=quarantine or p=reject, but a domain that's been sitting on p=none for years isn't actually protected against spoofing.

Why isn't there a single authoritative list of DKIM selectors?

Unlike SPF and DMARC, which live at fixed, predictable record names, a DKIM selector is chosen by whoever configured the mail-sending service and isn't published anywhere in DNS on its own. The selector lookup tries common defaults used by major providers (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and generic setups) plus anything specific you supply.

Why bother identifying verification TXT records?

Domains that have been through multiple SaaS onboardings accumulate TXT records like google-site-verification=... or facebook-domain-verification=... that nobody remembers adding. Before deleting anything during a DNS cleanup, it helps to know which service a record belongs to and whether that integration is still in use.

Is anything sent to a server?

Domain names you enter are sent to the DNS-over-HTTPS resolver you select in settings (Cloudflare or Google) to perform the lookup — that's the only network activity. All parsing (SPF, DMARC, verification-tag matching) happens locally in your browser. Nothing is logged by this tool itself, and your settings only persist in this browser's local storage.
