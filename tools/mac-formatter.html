✦ How This Tool Works

What problem does this actually solve?

MAC addresses show up in a dozen inconsistent notations across switches, packet captures, DHCP leases, and vendor spec sheets. This tool normalizes any of them, shows every common representation, classifies the address by its control bits, flags reserved multicast ranges before they're mistaken for real devices, and audits whole lists at once — all client-side.

What's the I/G bit and the U/L bit?

The least-significant bit of the first octet is the Individual/Group (I/G) bit: 0 means the frame is addressed to a single device (unicast), 1 means it's addressed to a group (multicast or broadcast). The next bit is the Universal/Local (U/L) bit: 0 means the address was burned in by the vendor using their assigned OUI, 1 means it was set locally — common for VMs, containers, and randomized Wi-Fi MACs.

Why do some multicast MACs get a special label?

Certain multicast ranges are reserved by standards bodies rather than assigned to a vendor: 01:80:C2 is used by Spanning Tree and other 802.1 bridge protocols, 01:00:0C covers Cisco's CDP/VTP/PAgP frames, 01:00:5E is the mapping IPv4 multicast uses at Layer 2, and 33:33 is the equivalent for IPv6 multicast. Recognizing these avoids mistaking protocol control traffic for a rogue device.

How is the EUI-64 / link-local address derived?

RFC 4291's modified EUI-64 process splits the 48-bit MAC into two 24-bit halves, inserts fffe between them to make a 64-bit interface identifier, and flips the U/L bit so a globally unique (vendor-assigned) MAC produces a globally unique identifier. Prefixing that identifier with fe80::/64 gives the interface's automatically-configured IPv6 link-local address.

Is anything sent to a server?

No, with one exception: vendor lookup is opt-in and, when enabled, sends the address's OUI to api.macvendors.com to identify the manufacturer. Everything else — parsing, reformatting, classification, EUI-64 derivation, batch auditing — runs entirely in your browser. Your draft and settings only persist in this browser's local storage.

Can I clean up a messy list of MACs pulled from a switch?

Paste the whole list into the input box, pick your target separator and case in Output Format, and switch to the Batch Table tab — it validates every line, flags duplicates and malformed entries, and the Download Batch CSV button exports a normalized version ready to re-import elsewhere.
