✦ How This Tool Works

Where does the "risk" classification come from?

Two levels: caution means the service itself is legitimate but is a frequent target for misconfiguration, brute-forcing, or exploitation when exposed to the wrong network (RDP, SMB, Redis, Elasticsearch, and similar). Malware means the port has little or no legitimate common use and is strongly associated with a known backdoor, trojan, or botnet C2 channel (31337, 12345, 4444, and similar). Neither label means a port is inherently dangerous by itself — context (what's actually listening, and to whom it's exposed) always matters more than the number alone.

What formats does the scan annotator accept?

Full nmap-style lines (22/tcp open ssh), a bare protocol/port pair, a bare port number, or a comma-separated list of numbers. When a line has no protocol attached, it's treated as TCP by default, or checked as both TCP and UDP if that setting is turned on above.

Why does the firewall generator support five different formats?

A rule that's correct syntax for iptables is meaningless to a Cisco router or a Windows box, and copy-pasting between them by hand is a common source of typos in an otherwise correct security change. Picking the format up front means the output can go straight into a change request or a config file.

Is the port database exhaustive?

No single free list ever is \u2014 IANA's official registry alone has tens of thousands of entries, most of them registered but never actually observed in the wild. This database favors the ~150 ports you'll actually encounter doing real network, sysadmin, or security work: standard services, common database and dev-tool defaults, industrial/ICS protocols, and the handful of ports with well-documented malware history.

My scan found a port with no match \u2014 what does that mean?

An unmatched port isn't necessarily suspicious \u2014 it could be a registered port this database doesn't cover, or an application listening on a nonstandard port on purpose. Treat "no match" as a prompt to identify the service manually (banner-grabbing or checking the process bound to that port), not as an automatic red flag.

Why does port 20000 list two unrelated services?

Ports above 1023 are only IANA-registered, not reserved \u2014 nothing stops two different products from picking the same number, and in practice several do (20000 is both a common Usermin panel port and one of the ports used by the DNP3 industrial protocol). When a lookup is ambiguous like this, the database says so rather than guessing.
