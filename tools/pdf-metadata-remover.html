✦ How This Tool Works

What's actually hiding inside a PDF?

Two separate metadata stores: the legacy Document Info dictionary (Author, Producer, dates, arbitrary custom keys apps like Word or Acrobat write silently) and the newer XMP packet, an XML block that can carry GPS coordinates, full edit/revision history, and software fingerprints — often duplicating or outliving what "Remove Personal Information" tools clear.

Why is there a risk score?

Not every field is equally sensitive — a Title is usually harmless, but Author, GPS coordinates, and custom tracking IDs can deanonymize you. The score weighs what was actually found by severity so you know at a glance whether a file is safe to send as-is.

Why verify after cleaning?

Some producers duplicate a field across both the Info dictionary and XMP — clearing only one leaves the other intact. Re-scanning the cleaned output and diffing it against the original confirms nothing survived, rather than just trusting the strip operation ran.

Can I rewrite fields instead of just deleting them?

Yes — after stripping, you can set your own Title, Author, Subject, and Producer values, add custom Info dictionary entries, or stamp a fixed/blank date. Useful for corporate templates that need a consistent Producer string rather than an empty one.

What does "Flag embedded JavaScript" do?

It doesn't strip anything by default — it inspects the file for /JavaScript and /JS actions and reports them, since embedded scripts are a code-execution risk that a metadata scrub alone won't catch. Treat a flagged file as needing manual review.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. Every PDF you load is scanned and cleaned locally in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere, which matters most for exactly the sensitive documents this tool is built for. Reload the page and everything is cleared except your settings.
