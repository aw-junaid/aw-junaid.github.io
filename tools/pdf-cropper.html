PDF Cropper

Scanned books ship with crooked margins, exports carry a stray watermark strip, and print files need a hairline trimmed for bleed. Set exact margins, auto-detect the real content box, drag a crop by eye, or split a two-page book scan into singles — page-range aware, batch-ready, and previewed before you commit.
Soft crop vs hard crop — know which one you need

A "soft" crop only changes the PDF's CropBox — the visible window — and is fully reversible, but some older viewers and print pipelines ignore CropBox and show the full page anyway. A "hard" crop also rewrites the MediaBox, physically discarding whatever falls outside it — permanent, but guaranteed to render cropped everywhere, including for removing a watermark strip you actually want gone.

✦ How This Tool Works

What's the difference between CropBox and MediaBox?

MediaBox is the full physical page; CropBox is the window a viewer shows by default. Setting only CropBox is non-destructive — the original content is still there, just outside the visible window. "Hard crop" also shrinks MediaBox, which permanently discards anything outside it and guarantees consistent behavior across viewers and print pipelines that ignore CropBox.

How does auto-detect find the content box?

Each page renders to an offscreen canvas, then the tool scans rows and columns for the first and last pixel darker than your whiteness threshold — that bounding box, plus your padding, becomes the crop. It's a per-page best-effort scan, not OCR, so a faint watermark or a stray scanner speck near the edge can throw it off; check the preview before committing.

Why drag on the preview instead of typing numbers?

Scan margins and watermark placement are rarely uniform enough to guess in points or millimeters on the first try. Drawing directly on the rendered page captures the box as a fraction of that page's size, so it's easy to eyeball correctly and it still scales sensibly if pages in the batch are different sizes.

What does Split Two-Up actually produce?

Each source page — typically a two-page book spread scanned as one image — is duplicated into two output pages, one showing only the left/top half and one showing only the right/bottom half, each with its own tightened MediaBox. The gutter trim shaves a small strip near the fold where binding shadow usually falls.

Can I apply different margins to a subset of pages?

Yes — Page Range restricts the crop to all pages, a custom list like 1-3,5,8-10, or odd/even only, and "Mirror margins on even pages" swaps left/right specifically for even pages, which matches how binding margins alternate in a scanned book.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. Every PDF you load is rendered, analyzed, and cropped locally in your browser using pdf.js and pdf-lib — nothing is sent anywhere, which matters most for exactly the sensitive or unpublished documents this tool tends to get used on.
