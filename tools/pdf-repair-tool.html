PDF Repair Tool

A truncated download, a crashed export, a byte flipped in transit — and suddenly a PDF won't open, or opens with blank pages. This scans the file with two independent parsers to pinpoint exactly what's broken (header, xref/trailer, page tree, individual pages), then rebuilds it: page-by-page recovery so one bad page doesn't sink the whole file, with image rasterization as a last resort when a page can't be salvaged any other way.
Why a PDF stops opening

Most "corrupted PDF" errors trace back to a handful of causes: a download that got cut off mid-transfer (missing %%EOF), a broken cross-reference table or trailer from a crashed export, a page tree with a dangling reference, or actual byte corruption from a bad upload/email gateway. Standard PDF readers give up on the whole file the moment one of these is wrong — this tool tries to recover everything that's still intact instead.

✦ How This Tool Works

What actually breaks inside a PDF?

Usually one of a few things: a download or email transfer that cut off mid-file (missing %%EOF), a crashed export that left the cross-reference table or trailer half-written, a page tree with a reference pointing at nothing, or flat-out byte corruption from a lossy transfer. Readers are unforgiving — one bad pointer and the whole file refuses to open, even though 99% of the content is intact.

Why scan with two separate parsers?

pdf-lib and PDF.js are independent implementations with different tolerances — pdf-lib is lenient about structure and good at rebuilding it, PDF.js is closer to what Acrobat and browsers actually render with. Cross-checking both catches page-level problems that either parser alone would silently accept or silently choke on.

What does page-by-page recovery actually do?

Instead of re-saving the damaged document as one unit — where a single broken page can fail the whole save — it builds a brand-new document and copies each page across individually, catching and logging any page that fails on its own rather than losing everything after it.

What is the image-based last-resort recovery?

When a page's underlying content stream is too damaged to copy as real PDF content, this renders that page with the independent PDF.js engine and embeds the result as a flattened image page instead. You keep what the page looked like; you lose selectable text and searchability on that specific page.

Can this repair a PDF that needs a password to open?

No — files encrypted with a user (open) password require that password to decrypt before any repair is possible, and this tool doesn't attempt to guess or crack it. It can bypass owner-only restrictions (files that open freely but block printing/editing), which is a different, much weaker protection.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. Every PDF you load is diagnosed and repaired locally in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere, which matters most for exactly the sensitive documents people are trying to recover. Reload the page and everything is cleared except your settings.
